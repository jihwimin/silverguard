SECTION 1: VOICE PHISHING (VISHING) OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Definition under Illinois and Federal Law:
Voice phishing is a form of fraud where criminals impersonate legitimate entities 
(banks, government agencies, tech support) via telephone to deceive victims into:
  • Revealing banking credentials or account numbers
  • Transferring money to fraudulent accounts
  • Authorizing unauthorized transactions
  • Sharing personal identifying information (SSN, ID photos, etc.)

Legal Classification:
  • Federal Wire Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343): Using interstate phone communications 
    to perpetrate fraud. Penalties: up to 20 years federal prison, $1 million fine.
  • Federal Bank Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1344): Fraud targeting financial institutions. 
    Penalties: up to 30 years federal prison, $1 million fine.
  • Illinois Criminal Theft/Fraud (720 ILCS 5/17-1, 5/17-2): State-level charges 
    for bank fraud or possession of fraudulently obtained funds.
  • Illinois Consumer Fraud Act (815 ILCS 505): Unfair/deceptive practices. 
    Victims may seek civil damages including treble damages.

Sources:
  - 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud)
  - 18 U.S.C. § 1344 (Bank Fraud)
  - 720 ILCS 5/17-1 (Illinois Bank Fraud)
  - 815 ILCS 505 (Illinois Consumer Fraud Act)

SECTION 2: IMMEDIATE RESPONSE (First 24-72 Hours)
================================================================================

CRITICAL: Speed matters. Financial fraud recovery depends heavily on reporting 
within narrow time windows.

2.1 STOP THE TRANSFER (If Money Left Your Account)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Timing: Report IMMEDIATELY - within 24-72 hours if possible.

Federal Fraud Kill Chain (FFKC) Process:
The FBI, Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), and international banking 
networks operate a "Financial Fraud Kill Chain" to stop transfers BEFORE they reach 
their final destination. This only works within approximately 72 hours of the 
initial wire/transfer.

What to do:
1. Contact your bank's OFFICIAL customer service line (use number on your bank card 
   or official website - NOT any number the scammer provided).
2. Report the fraudulent transfer and request an IMMEDIATE ACCOUNT FREEZE on any 
   suspect transactions.
3. Provide the bank with:
   - Exact transfer amount
   - Date/time of transfer
   - Recipient account number (if you have it)
   - Recipient bank name/routing number (if known)
   - Method used (wire, ACH, P2P payment app like Venmo/PayPal, etc.)

Bank's Legal Obligation (EFTA/Regulation E):
Under the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (15 U.S.C. § 1693, Regulation E per 12 CFR 
1005), banks are required to:
  • Investigate error/fraud claims within 10 business days
  • Provide provisional credit during investigation
  • Report findings within 3 business days of investigation completion
  • NOT require police report as prerequisite to investigation (per 2025 CFPB 
    guidance)

Note: Traditional wire transfers from bank accounts are partially exempt from EFTA 
protection (no liability caps), BUT recent case law (New York v. Citibank, 2025) 
suggests wire transfers initiated through online banking platforms may fall under 
EFTA protections.

2.2 REPORT TO LAW ENFORCEMENT
──────────────────────────────

FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3):
  • Website: www.ic3.gov
  • File online complaint with details of fraud
  • Receive IC3 complaint number for reference
  • FBI uses aggregated IC3 data to identify fraud patterns and coordinate with 
    FinCEN for FFKC activation

Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI):
  • Chicago Field Office: (312) 421-6700
  • Local FBI field office finder: www.fbi.gov/contact
  • Call after filing IC3 report; provide IC3 report number
  • FBI will coordinate with victim's bank and FinCEN if FFKC criteria are met

Local Police (Non-Emergency Line):
  • Call local police non-emergency line (NOT 911 unless immediate physical danger)
  • Request to file fraud report/incident report
  • Keep incident report number and police report document

Note: Police may be slow to investigate, but official incident report is needed 
for bank fraud investigations and potential civil claims.

Federal Trade Commission (FTC):
  • Website: reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • FTC aggregates fraud complaints; does not directly investigate but provides 
    enforcement support
  • Complaint helps identify patterns

2.3 DOCUMENT EVERYTHING
────────────────────────

Preserve evidence for law enforcement and banking investigation:
  • Screenshot any phone numbers displayed on caller ID
  • Save voicemails (forward to yourself via email if possible)
  • Document exact time/date of calls
  • Write down what the scammer said (recreate conversation if possible)
  • Keep all bank statements showing fraudulent transactions
  • Save any emails/texts from scammer
  • Note account numbers/transfer details provided to scammer (if any)

This documentation will be critical for:
  • Bank fraud investigation
  • FBI pursuit of FFKC process
  • Any civil or criminal proceedings

SECTION 3: BANK ERROR RESOLUTION PROCEDURES
================================================================================

Illinois and federal law (EFTA/Regulation E) establish specific procedures for 
disputing unauthorized transfers.

3.1 REPORTING UNAUTHORIZED TRANSFERS
──────────────────────────────────────

Reporting Deadline (CRITICAL):
  • You have 60 days from the date your bank statement was MAILED/PROVIDED to 
    report an unauthorized transfer
  • Time limit begins on first statement showing the unauthorized transaction
  • Missing 60-day deadline = bank has NO legal obligation to investigate

How to Report:
  • Call bank's official customer service number (on card or official website)
  • Oral notification is sufficient to trigger investigation
  • Bank may require written confirmation within 10 days of oral notice
  • But investigation timeline starts from oral notice date, not written confirmation

Required Information to Provide:
  • Your full name and account number
  • Date(s) of unauthorized transaction(s)
  • Amount(s) of transfer(s)
  • Clear statement: "This transaction is unauthorized. I did not authorize this 
    transfer."

Legal References:
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1693e (EFTA: Error Resolution)
  - 12 CFR 1005.5 (Regulation E: Error Resolution Procedures)
  - 12 CFR 1005.2(m) (Definition of Unauthorized EFT)

3.2 BANK'S INVESTIGATION OBLIGATIONS
───────────────────────────────────────

Timeline:
  • Bank must begin investigation within 1 business day of oral notification
  • Investigation must be completed within 10 business days
    (Can extend to 45 days if bank provides provisional credit immediately)
  • Bank must report findings in writing within 3 business days after investigation 
    completion

Investigation Requirements (Bank MUST):
  • NOT require police report as condition of investigation (CFPB 2025 guidance)
  • NOT use consumer negligence to deny claim (even if you were tricked into 
    providing credentials)
  • Investigate whether transfer was truly unauthorized
  • Verify account access circumstances
  • Determine if bank's security measures were breached

Provisional Credit:
  • If investigation extends beyond 10 days, bank must provide provisional credit 
    immediately (credited back to your account during investigation)
  • Provisional credit is NOT final decision - if bank's investigation later finds 
    transfer was authorized, bank can debit provisional credit back out

Liability Caps (for unauthorized EFTs under EFTA):
  Note: These caps may NOT apply to wire transfers made through online banking, 
  depending on recent court interpretations. Check with your specific bank.
  
  • If you report within 2 business days of first noticing: Liability capped at $50
  • If you report within 60 days: Liability capped at $500
  • If you report after 60 days: Bank has NO obligation; you could be liable for 
    full amount (depending on fraud type and bank's negligence)

Important: Consumer negligence (falling for scam, providing credentials) CANNOT 
be used to impose greater liability than above limits.

Legal References:
  - 12 CFR 1005.5 (Investigation Procedures)
  - 12 CFR 1005.6 (Liability Limits)
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1693f (Bank Error Correction)

3.3 IF BANK DENIES CLAIM
──────────────────────────

Civil Action Under EFTA:
If bank refuses to correct error or fails to follow investigation procedures, you 
may sue for:
  • Actual damages (loss amount)
  • Statutory damages: up to $1,000 (even if actual loss is less)
  • Treble damages (3x actual damages) if bank acted in bad faith
  • Reasonable attorney fees and costs

Requirements to Sue:
  • Bank violated EFTA/Regulation E procedures
  • Must file within 2 years of discovering violation (or 3 years for ongoing breach)
  • Federal court (applies to interstate transactions) or Illinois state court 
    (815 ILCS violations)

Illinois State Consumer Fraud Claim (Alternative):
Under Illinois Consumer Fraud Act (815 ILCS 505):
  • Deceptive or unfair business practice in trade/commerce
  • Damages: actual damages OR $200 per violation (whichever is greater)
  • Treble damages if defendant's conduct was willful/reckless
  • Attorney fees awarded to prevailing plaintiff

Legal References:
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1693m (EFTA: Liability of Financial Institutions)
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1693n (EFTA: Illegal Action)
  - 815 ILCS 505/2A (Illinois Consumer Fraud Act)

Consult Attorney:
Contact a consumer protection attorney in Illinois if bank denies reasonable fraud 
claim. Many work on contingency (no upfront cost).

SECTION 4: IDENTITY THEFT & CREDENTIAL COMPROMISE
================================================================================

If you revealed sensitive information to scammer, take additional steps:

4.1 PERSONAL INFORMATION EXPOSURE
──────────────────────────────────

Information at Risk (Common Scenarios):
  • Account numbers/routing numbers: Risk of unauthorized transfers
  • Online banking credentials (username/password): Risk of account takeover
  • Social Security Number: Risk of new credit accounts opened in your name
  • Government ID photos (driver's license, passport): Risk of identity fraud
  • Credit card numbers/expiration dates: Risk of unauthorized charges
  • Answers to security questions: Risk of account reset/takeover

Immediate Actions:
1. Change password on compromised account IMMEDIATELY (from different device/network 
   if possible)
2. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on all financial accounts if available
3. Monitor account statements closely for 30+ days for additional unauthorized 
   activity
4. If ID numbers compromised: Consider placing fraud alert or credit freeze

4.2 CREDIT MONITORING & FRAUD ALERTS
──────────────────────────────────────

Fraud Alert:
  • Temporary (1 year) flag on credit report
  • Creditors must verify identity before issuing new credit in your name
  • To request: Contact any of the 3 major credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, 
    TransUnion)
  • Free to place

Credit Freeze (Security Freeze):
  • Permanent restriction on who can access your credit file
  • Prevents criminals from opening accounts in your name
  • You must temporarily "thaw" to apply for legitimate credit
  • Illinois law allows free credit freeze (815 ILCS 505/2Q-2Q.1)
  • More restrictive than fraud alert but more protective

How to Place Freeze (Illinois):
  • Equifax: www.equifax.com
  • Experian: www.experian.com
  • TransUnion: www.transunion.com
  • Each bureau handles requests independently; do all three
  • Free under Illinois law (815 ILCS 505/2Q)

Check Credit Reports:
  • Get FREE annual credit report from each bureau: www.annualcreditreport.com
  • Review for suspicious accounts/inquiries
  • Dispute any fraudulent accounts

Illinois-Specific Resources:
  • Illinois Attorney General Cybercrime Division: www.cybercrime.illinois.gov
  • Report identity theft to AG's office for coordination

Legal References:
  - 815 ILCS 505/2Q (Illinois Security Freeze Law)
  - Federal law: 15 U.S.C. § 1681c (Fraud Alerts & Freezes)

SECTION 5: WIRE TRANSFER RECOVERY OPTIONS
================================================================================

5.1 FEDERAL FRAUD KILL CHAIN (FFKC)
────────────────────────────────────

Purpose:
The FFKC is a coordinated international process (FBI + FinCEN + foreign banking 
regulators) to STOP fraudulent wire transfers before they reach final destination 
and recover funds.

Eligibility Criteria (ALL must be met):
  • Transfer must be detected within ~72 hours of initiation
  • Wire/transfer must be international OR cross-state lines
  • Fraudulent nature must be verified
  • Victim bank must cooperate
  • Recipient bank must cooperate (foreign or domestic)

Activation Process:
1. File IC3 complaint at www.ic3.gov immediately
2. Contact FBI field office with IC3 complaint number
3. FBI contacts FinCEN with fraud details
4. FinCEN initiates international banking coordination
5. Receiving bank is notified to HOLD funds before settlement
6. If successful, funds are returned to victim (through series of transfers)

Success Rate:
  • ~50% of FFKC-initiated cases result in recovery if activated within 72 hours
  • Drops dramatically after 72 hours
  • Higher success for domestic transfers than international

Time-Critical Action:
If money was wire transferred or ACH'd out of your account, CALL YOUR BANK 
IMMEDIATELY and ask:
  • "Can you activate the Financial Fraud Kill Chain?"
  • "How long do I have to report for FFKC eligibility?"
  • Provide IC3 complaint number if already filed

Legal References:
  - 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (Wire Fraud - criminal basis for FBI action)
  - 31 U.S.C. § 5318 (FinCEN Authority)
  - Federal Reserve/OCC Guidance on Wire Fraud Response

Note: FFKC is not guaranteed. Speed and cooperation are essential. Many cases 
result in only partial recovery or no recovery.

5.2 CIVIL RECOVERY (If Criminal Recovery Fails)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────

Lawsuit Against Receiving Bank (in some cases):
Illinois law may allow civil suit against a receiving bank if:
  • Bank accepted deposits knowing (or should have known) they were proceeds of fraud
  • Bank failed to implement adequate fraud detection
  • Bank had suspicious activity indicators but processed anyway

Lawsuit Against Scammer (if identified):
  • Generally difficult - scammers operate anonymously/internationally
  • Judgment may be uncollectable
  • Possible in Illinois state court or federal court

Restitution in Criminal Case (if scammer is prosecuted):
  • If FBI identifies and prosecutes scammer, court may order restitution
  • Restitution collected by DOJ and distributed to victims
  • Takes years; low collection rate

Class Action Against Bank:
  • If multiple customers defrauded by same incident, class action may be possible
  • Contact consumer protection attorney

Consult Attorney:
Given complexity, consult Illinois consumer protection/banking lawyer for civil 
recovery options.

SECTION 6: P2P PAYMENT APP FRAUD (Venmo, PayPal, Square Cash, etc.)
================================================================================

Increasing risk: Scammers trick victims into sending money via P2P apps, which are 
harder to reverse than bank transfers.

6.1 IMMEDIATE REPORTING (P2P Apps)
───────────────────────────────────

P2P apps have limited fraud protection compared to banks.

Steps:
1. Report to the P2P app immediately:
   • Go to app settings → Report a Problem
   • Or contact app's fraud department
   • Provide transaction ID, recipient username, amount, date
2. Report to your bank (if linked debit/credit card):
   • Bank may reverse through underlying payment method
3. Report to FBI IC3: www.ic3.gov
4. File police report (local non-emergency)

Likelihood of Recovery:
  • 20-30% recovery rate (much lower than wire transfers)
  • P2P apps freeze suspect accounts but don't guarantee return
  • If recipient already withdrew cash, recovery unlikely

EFTA Protection on P2P:
  • P2P transfers initiated through linked debit accounts may have SOME EFTA 
    protection (recent CFPB guidance)
  • Credit card P2P: May fall under Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) instead
  • Terms vary by app; check app's terms

6.2 DISPUTE PROCEDURES (App-Specific)
──────────────────────────────────────

Venmo:
  • Dispute link: Settings → About Venmo → Contact Us → Report an Issue
  • Success rate: Low if recipient already transferred out

PayPal:
  • File dispute through Resolution Center
  • May offer buyer protection if linked to PayPal Wallet
  • But protection limited for person-to-person transfers

Square Cash (Cash App):
  • Report to Square through app
  • Very limited chargeback protections
  • Recommend contacting linked bank instead

Recommendation:
For all P2P fraud, contact underlying bank first (if debit card linked) as bank 
has better fraud reversal power than P2P app itself.

SECTION 7: CREDIT CARD FRAUD (If Scammer Used Card or Opened New Card)
================================================================================

7.1 UNAUTHORIZED CREDIT CARD CHARGES
──────────────────────────────────────

Liability:
  • Federal law (Fair Credit Billing Act - FCBA, 15 U.S.C. § 1666): You are liable 
    for ONLY $50 maximum per card for unauthorized charges
  • Visa/Mastercard rules: Often zero liability for cardholders

Reporting:
  • Call credit card issuer IMMEDIATELY upon noticing unauthorized charge
  • Provide card number, charge amount, date, merchant
  • Request dispute/chargeback process
  • Card issuer must investigate within 30-60 days
  • Issuer must credit back disputed amount while investigating

7.2 FRAUDULENT ACCOUNT OPENING (Credit Fraud)
───────────────────────────────────────────────

If scammer opened NEW credit card/account in your name:

Immediate Actions:
1. Contact credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)
2. Place fraud alert or credit freeze on your file
3. Request credit reports to identify unauthorized accounts
4. Dispute fraudulent accounts with credit card issuer directly:
   - Provide written statement that account is fraudulent
   - Attach police report or IC3 report
   - Credit card issuer must investigate (dispute process)
5. Report to Federal Trade Commission identity theft division: 
   www.identitytheft.gov (creates recovery plan)

Illinois ID Theft Law:
  • 815 ILCS 530: Illinois Identity Theft Statute
  • Victims have civil right to sue for damages
  • Attorney General can prosecute criminal identity theft
  • Victims can demand identity theft affidavit from businesses

Legal References:
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1666 (Fair Credit Billing Act)
  - 815 ILCS 530 (Illinois Identity Theft Act)
  - 16 CFR Part 682 (FTC ID Theft Rules)

SECTION 8: POLICE REPORT & EVIDENCE
================================================================================

8.1 OBTAINING POLICE REPORT
─────────────────────────────

Why It Matters:
  • Banks may request police report to corroborate fraud claim (though legally 
    cannot REQUIRE it per 2025 CFPB guidance)
  • FBI may use incident report to verify FFKC eligibility
  • Evidence for civil lawsuit if pursuing private action
  • Establishes official record

How to File (Illinois):
  • Call local police non-emergency line (NOT 911)
  • Go to local police station and request to file report
  • Provide details: dates, phone numbers, amounts, what you told scammer
  • Request written incident report and incident number
  • Keep copy for records

Challenges:
  • Police may be reluctant to investigate (no property recovered yet)
  • Tell police: "I want to file a report for fraud/wire fraud/bank fraud"
  • Incident number is what matters - police investigation may be minimal

Alternative: FBI as Lead Agency
  • FBI has primary jurisdiction over wire fraud/bank fraud
  • FBI typically takes over investigation from local police
  • File IC3 report (www.ic3.gov) to trigger FBI interest
  • IC3 complaint number sometimes sufficient without police report

8.2 EVIDENCE PRESERVATION
──────────────────────────

Critical Evidence to Keep:
  1. Phone records (itemized bill showing scammer's phone number, call dates/times)
  2. Screenshots of caller ID
  3. Voicemails (save audio files)
  4. Bank statements (showing transfers out)
  5. Screenshots of P2P app transactions (if applicable)
  6. Emails/texts from scammer (if any)
  7. Your own notes (dates, times, what was said)
  8. Fraud documentation (IC3 receipt, police report, bank investigation letter)

How to Preserve:
  • Print or screenshot everything
  • Save copies to personal files/cloud storage
  • Do NOT delete anything from phone/email (may need for investigation)
  • Create organized file with dates/descriptions

SECTION 9: SPECIAL SITUATIONS
================================================================================

9.1 REAL ESTATE OR LARGE PURCHASE SCAMS
─────────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer impersonates real estate agent/attorney, requests wire for 
closing costs.

Response:
  1. STOP - Verify independently through official channels
     • Call real estate office directly using number on website
     • Call attorney's office directly using number from bar association
     • Never use contact info provided by "agent"
  2. Wire Fraud: 18 U.S.C. § 1343 (federal crime)
     • Report to FBI immediately
     • FFKC likely applicable if large amount and quick reporting
  3. Try to recall wire BEFORE it settles:
     • Call bank IMMEDIATELY (real estate closings often occur same-day)
     • Request wire reversal/recall
     • May be possible if recipient bank hasn't settled
  4. File IC3 complaint + police report

Likelihood of Recovery:
  • 40-60% if caught within 24-48 hours
  • Much lower after settlement (wire is nearly irreversible once settled)

9.2 GOVERNMENT/SOCIAL SECURITY IMPERSONATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer claims to be IRS, SSA, or law enforcement, threatening arrest 
unless payment made.

Legal Framework:
  • 18 U.S.C. § 911: Impersonating government officer (federal crime)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1343: Wire fraud (if using phone)
  • Illinois § 5/17-1: State fraud statute

Red Flags (NEVER real):
  • Government agencies do NOT call threatening arrest
  • IRS does NOT demand immediate wire transfer
  • Social Security does NOT threaten benefit cancellation
  • Police do NOT threaten arrest over phone

Response:
  1. Hang up immediately
  2. Do NOT provide any information or make any transfer
  3. If transferred money: Follow standard recovery procedures (Section 3)
  4. Report to FBI, IC3, local police
  5. Report to impersonated agency:
     • IRS: treasury.gov/fraud
     • Social Security: ssa.gov/fraud
     • USCIS: dhs.gov/report

Recovery Outlook:
  • Extremely difficult - these scams are international
  • Focus on bank/FFKC recovery (Section 5) rather than scammer prosecution

10.2 VICTIM SUPPORT
────────────────────

Emotional Impact:
Fraud victimization is traumatic. Consider:
  • Talking to trusted friends/family
  • Consulting therapist or counselor
  • Support groups for fraud victims (some nonprofit organizations offer)

Legal Support:
  • Legal aid organizations (if low-income)

  • Review bank statements weekly for unauthorized activity
  • Update passwords and security questions
  • Enable multi-factor authentication everywhere possible

SECTION 12: GLOSSARY OF LEGAL/FINANCIAL TERMS
================================================================================

ACH (Automated Clearing House)
  Electronic interbank transfer system. Slower than wire transfers (1-3 business 
  days). Some EFTA protections apply.

CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau)
  Federal agency overseeing consumer financial protection. Enforces EFTA.

Chargeback
  Dispute mechanism where credit card issuer reverses transaction and investigates.

EFTA (Electronic Fund Transfer Act)
  Federal law (15 U.S.C. § 1693) protecting consumers in electronic fund transfers.

FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network)
  U.S. Treasury agency coordinating international wire fraud prevention.

FFKC (Financial Fraud Kill Chain)
  FBI/FinCEN coordination to stop fraudulent wire transfers before settlement.

IC3 (Internet Crime Complaint Center)
  FBI's public-facing fraud complaint portal.

Regulation E (12 CFR Part 1005)
  Federal regulations implementing EFTA.

Wire Transfer
  Fast (usually same-day) transfer of funds between banks via SWIFT/Fedwire networks. 
  Limited EFTA protection (though recent court cases are expanding this).

Vishing
  Voice phishing - fraud via telephone calls.

Unauthorized Transfer
  Electronic fund transfer initiated without actual authority of account holder.

SECTION 13: SUMMARY FOR CHATBOT RESPONSES
================================================================================

When responding to voice phishing victims:

IMMEDIATE RESPONSE:
1. Acknowledge victim is not alone; scammers are sophisticated
2. Direct to official channels FIRST:
   - Their bank (official customer service number)
   - FBI IC3 (www.ic3.gov)
   - Local police (non-emergency line)
3. Emphasize: Speed matters (72-hour FFKC window)

GENERAL INFORMATION:
- Provide law references (not specific legal advice)
- Direct to official government/bank resources
- Recommend consulting licensed attorney for civil claims
- Explain EFTA protections in plain language
- Clarify common misconceptions (bank CANNOT require police report)

AVOID:
- Predicting recovery probability (depends on many factors)
- Specific legal advice ("You should sue..." or "You should claim...")
- Detailed personal financial advice
- Technical banking procedures (varies by institution)

ALWAYS CONCLUDE:
"For specific guidance on your situation, contact:
  • Your bank's official fraud department
  • FBI field office (www.fbi.gov/contact)
  • Licensed consumer protection attorney in Illinois"
