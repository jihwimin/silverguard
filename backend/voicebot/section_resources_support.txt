SECTION 8: PSYCHOLOGICAL SUPPORT & RESOURCES
================================================================================

Important: Many victims experience shame, embarrassment, or depression after 
voice phishing. This is normal. Scammers are highly trained social engineers.

8.1 REPORTING RESOURCES
─────────────────────────

Federal Agencies:
  • FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center: www.ic3.gov
    - Online complaint form
    - Receives IC3 complaint number for reference
    - FBI uses data for pattern analysis and criminal investigation

  • Federal Trade Commission: reportfraud.ftc.gov
    - FTC aggregates fraud complaints
    - Does not directly investigate but provides enforcement support
    - Complaint helps identify national patterns

  • Consumer Financial Protection Bureau: www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint
    - Submit complaint about financial institution
    - CFPB takes enforcement action against institutions

  • U.S. Attorney General Fraud Hotline: (866) 720-5721
    - Referral service for federal fraud complaints

Illinois-Specific:
  • Illinois Attorney General Cybercrime Division: www.cybercrime.illinois.gov
    - Handle Illinois state-level cybercrime
    - Prosecute state criminal fraud cases
    - Can provide resources to victims

  • Illinois Attorney General Fraud Bureau: (877) 424-7000
    - Phone hotline for reporting fraud
    - Provides victim guidance
    - May coordinate state/federal investigation

  • Chicago Police Department Non-Emergency: (311) or (312) 746-6000
    - File local incident report
    - Required for many insurance/bank claims

Local Police:
  • Call non-emergency line (311 in Chicago)
  • Go to local police station to file report
  • Get incident report number and copy
  • Keep for bank/civil proceedings

Your Financial Institution:
  • Contact bank's official fraud department
    (Number on back of card or official website)
  • Report unauthorized transaction
  • Request investigation
  • Ask about FFKC eligibility (wire transfers)

8.2 VICTIM SUPPORT & COUNSELING
─────────────────────────────────

Emotional Impact (Normal Reactions):
  • Shame/embarrassment (common - scammers are professionals)
  • Anxiety/hypervigilance (normal after financial trauma)
  • Depression/hopelessness (especially after large loss)
  • Loss of trust (difficulty with financial decisions)
  • Self-blame ("I should have known better")

Getting Help:
  • Talk to trusted friends/family
  • Consider therapy/counseling (many therapists specialize in financial trauma)
  • Join support groups (online fraud victim groups available)
  • Contact community mental health services (often free/low-cost)
  • SAMHSA National Helpline: (800) 662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7)

Illinois Resources:
  • Illinois Department of Human Services: Mental health services directory
  • Community mental health centers: Offer sliding-scale counseling
  • University counseling centers: Often provide community services
  • Crisis line: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (text or call)

Online Support Communities:
  • Reddit: r/personalfinance (fraud recovery advice)
  • Scam survivor forums: Many nonprofit organizations host communities
  • State AG victim assistance programs: Free resources

Why It's Not Your Fault:
  • Scammers are trained social engineers (this is their job)
  • They have sophisticated scripts perfected over years
  • They exploit trust and natural human psychology
  • Falling for a scam does NOT indicate stupidity or poor judgment
  • Victims come from all backgrounds/education levels

8.3 LEGAL & FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE
──────────────────────────────────

Legal Aid Organizations:
  • Illinois Legal Aid Online: www.cybercrime.illinois.gov (victim resources)
  • Chicago Bar Association Lawyer Referral: www.chicagobar.org
  • Low-income legal aid: Contact local legal aid society
  • Many offer free initial consultation
  • Some work on contingency (no upfront cost)

Consumer Protection Attorneys:
  • Specialize in EFTA violations and bank fraud
  • Many offer free initial consultation
  • Typically work on contingency basis
  • Can file civil suit against bank/issuer if needed
  • Illinois State Bar Association: www.isba.org (find attorney)

Credit Counseling Services:
  • NFCC (National Foundation for Credit Counseling): www.nfcc.org
    - Free/low-cost counseling
    - Help with financial recovery planning
    - Credit management advice

Financial Recovery Planning:
  • Create realistic recovery timeline
  • Budget for monitoring services
  • Plan for credit monitoring (12+ months)
  • Document everything for potential civil claims
  • Consult tax professional if fraud impacts taxes

8.4 LONG-TERM MONITORING & PREVENTION
───────────────────────────────────────

First 90 Days (Critical):
  • Review statements weekly (not monthly)
  • Check credit report monthly (all three bureaus)
  • Monitor for additional unauthorized activity
  • Watch for new account opening attempts
  • Track all communications with banks/authorities

First 12 Months:
  • Continue monthly credit monitoring
  • Update passwords regularly
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (2FA) everywhere
  • Monitor tax return status (fraudsters file false returns)
  • Watch for suspicious loan/credit applications
  • Continue therapy/support if helpful

Long-term Prevention:
  • Use strong, unique passwords for each account
  • Enable biometric security (fingerprint, face recognition)
  • Verify phone numbers independently (don't trust caller ID)
  • Never give account info over phone (unsolicited calls)
  • Use password managers (e.g., 1Password, LastPass)
  • Consider credit freeze (permanent protection)
  • Keep emergency fund separate (for unexpected issues)

8.5 DOCUMENTATION FOR CIVIL CLAIMS
────────────────────────────────────

What To Keep (For Potential Lawsuit):
  • All bank statements showing fraudulent transactions
  • Police incident report & report number
  • FBI IC3 complaint number & confirmation
  • Bank investigation letter & results
  • Screenshots of fraudulent calls/emails
  • Phone records showing scammer's number
  • Your written account of what happened (dates, amounts, statements made)
  • Any communications from bank (emails, letters)
  • Evidence of losses (bank statements, credit monitoring costs, etc.)
  • Proof of good faith reporting (IC3 receipt, police report)

Organization Tips:
  • Create folder (digital/physical) with label "Fraud Documentation"
  • Organize chronologically (dates matter for statutes of limitation)
  • Include copies of all communications
  • Keep originals (don't mail unless required)
  • Backup digital files to cloud storage
  • Share with attorney if filing suit

Statute of Limitations (when you can sue):
  • EFTA violations: 2 years of discovery (or 3 years for ongoing breach)
  • Illinois Consumer Fraud: 3 years
  • Federal Wire Fraud (criminal): No limit (but FBI may not prosecute)
  • Don't wait - file within 2 years if considering lawsuit
