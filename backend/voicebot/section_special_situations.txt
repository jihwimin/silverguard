SECTION 7: SPECIAL SITUATIONS

7.1 REAL ESTATE OR LARGE PURCHASE SCAMS
─────────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer impersonates real estate agent/attorney, requests wire for 
closing costs.

Red Flags:
  • Agent/attorney asks you to wire money without meeting in person
  • Email address slightly different from official firm email
  • Phone number different from published office number
  • Request to wire to new/unfamiliar bank account
  • Urgency: "We need this by end of business today"
  • Unusual: Attorney/agent usually handles closing account transfers

Legal Framework:
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1343: Wire Fraud (federal crime, up to 20 years)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1344: Bank Fraud (federal crime, up to 30 years)
  • Common in Chicago/suburban real estate transactions (high-value targets)

What To Do (If You Already Wired):
  1. STOP - Verify independently through official channels
     • Call real estate office directly using number on website (NOT from "agent")
     • Call attorney's office directly using number from bar association/website
     • Ask: "Did you request a wire for closing?"
  2. Wire Fraud Report: Call FBI immediately
     • FBI Chicago Field Office: (312) 421-6700
     • File IC3 complaint: www.ic3.gov
  3. Try to recall wire BEFORE it settles:
     • Call bank IMMEDIATELY (real estate closings often occur same-day)
     • Request wire reversal/recall
     • May be possible if recipient bank hasn't settled
     • Mention "FFKC activation" to bank
  4. File police report and IC3 complaint
  5. Contact title company/real estate office about fraud

Likelihood of Recovery:
  • 40-60% if caught within 24-48 hours
  • Much lower after settlement (wire is nearly irreversible once settled)
  • High-value wires have better recovery chances (higher priority)

Prevention (For Future):
  • NEVER wire closing costs based on email request
  • Always call attorney/agent directly using published phone number
  • Attend closing in person if possible
  • Verify wire instructions in writing from official email
  • Use attorney-held escrow accounts (standard practice)
  • Ask title company to handle funds transfer (safer)

7.2 GOVERNMENT/SOCIAL SECURITY IMPERSONATION
───────────────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer claims to be IRS, SSA, or law enforcement, threatening arrest 
unless payment made.

Legal Framework:
  • 18 U.S.C. § 911: Impersonating government officer (federal crime)
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1343: Wire fraud (if using phone/internet)
  • 720 ILCS 5/17-36: Illinois impersonating peace officer statute
  • Common in Chicago/Midwest (high-volume fraud)

Red Flags (NEVER Real):
  • Government agencies do NOT call threatening arrest
  • IRS does NOT demand immediate wire transfer
  • IRS does NOT accept iTunes cards or Google Play cards as payment
  • Social Security does NOT threaten benefit cancellation via phone
  • Police do NOT threaten arrest over phone for debt
  • Government does NOT request personal info via phone
  • Social Security does NOT ask for password/PIN
  • Legitimate agencies send written notices FIRST

What Government Really Does:
  • IRS: Sends written notice via mail (certified if serious)
  • SSA: Sends written notice; does NOT threaten benefits by phone
  • Police: Arrest in person or send written summons
  • Government: Never asks for immediate payment

What To Do If Targeted:
  1. Hang up immediately
  2. Do NOT provide any information or make any transfer
  3. Do NOT believe threat of arrest (scam tactic)
  4. If transferred money: Follow standard recovery procedures (Sections 2-5)
  5. Report to FBI, IC3, local police
  6. Report to impersonated agency:
     • IRS: treasury.gov/fraud
     • Social Security: ssa.gov/fraud
     • USCIS: dhs.gov/report
     • FBI: www.ic3.gov

7.3 TECH SUPPORT / RANSOMWARE SCAMS
─────────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer claims to be Microsoft/Apple support, says your computer is 
infected, demands payment for "removal."

Red Flags:
  • Unsolicited call claiming to be tech support
  • Pop-up on computer saying "Your PC is infected"
  • Microsoft/Apple would NOT call you (they don't initiate support calls)
  • Request for remote access to your computer
  • Demand for payment via gift cards/wire transfer
  • Pressure: "Your data will be deleted if you don't act now"

Prevention:
  • Microsoft/Apple NEVER call unsolicited
  • Pop-ups on computer are scams
  • Never give remote access to unknown callers
  • Legitimate support companies accept credit cards (not gift cards)

If You Already Paid:
  • Report to FBI IC3: www.ic3.gov
  • Contact your bank for chargeback (if credit card)
  • File local police report
  • If gift card was purchased: Contact card issuer (may be recoverable)
  • If remote access given: Run antivirus scan, change passwords

7.4 LOTTERY/PRIZE WINNING SCAMS
─────────────────────────────────

Scenario: Scammer claims you won lottery/prize you didn't enter, demands upfront 
payment or wire for taxes/fees.

Red Flag:
  • You didn't enter the contest
  • Legitimate prizes NEVER require upfront payment
  • You must wire money "for taxes" (scam indicator)
  • Urgent pressure to claim prize

Legal Reality:
  • If you didn't enter = you didn't win
  • Legitimate lotteries send official government notices
  • Taxes are paid from prize (not by winner upfront)
  • This is 100% scam with 0% real winnings

If You Already Paid:
  • Report to FBI IC3: www.ic3.gov
  • Contact your bank for dispute/chargeback
  • File local police report
  • Unfortunately, recovery rate is very low

  • Official website: www.cybercrime.illinois.gov

================================================================================
KEY POINT: IF GOVERNMENT THREATENS YOU BY PHONE, IT'S A SCAM
================================================================================

Legitimate government agencies:
- Send written notice FIRST (certified mail)
- Have official processes (court orders, etc.)
- Do NOT demand immediate payment
- Do NOT ask for payment via gift cards or wire transfer
- Do NOT threaten immediate arrest

If threatened over phone: Hang up and call the agency's official number yourself
to verify (use number from official website, not one given by caller).
