SECTION 6: CREDIT CARD FRAUD (If Scammer Used Card or Opened New Card)
================================================================================

6.1 UNAUTHORIZED CREDIT CARD CHARGES
──────────────────────────────────────

Liability:
  • Federal law (Fair Credit Billing Act - FCBA, 15 U.S.C. § 1666): You are liable 
    for ONLY $50 maximum per card for unauthorized charges
  • Visa/Mastercard rules: Often zero liability for cardholders
  • In practice: Most issuers don't charge cardholders for fraudulent charges

Reporting:
  • Call credit card issuer IMMEDIATELY upon noticing unauthorized charge
  • Provide card number, charge amount, date, merchant
  • Request dispute/chargeback process
  • Card issuer must investigate within 30-60 days
  • Issuer must credit back disputed amount while investigating (provisional credit)

FCBA Protection Timeline:
  • Report within 60 days of billing statement date = Protected
  • Report after 60 days = No protection (you liable for full amount)
  • Use statement date, not transaction date (statements may arrive weeks late)

Investigation Process:
  • Issuer must send written acknowledgment within 30 days of receiving dispute
  • Full investigation must complete within 2 billing cycles (typically 60 days)
  • Issuer must credit provisional amount during investigation
  • Final decision sent to you in writing

6.2 FRAUDULENT ACCOUNT OPENING (Credit Fraud)
───────────────────────────────────────────────

If scammer opened NEW credit card/account in your name:

Immediate Actions:
1. Contact credit bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)
2. Place fraud alert or credit freeze on your file
3. Request credit reports to identify unauthorized accounts
4. Dispute fraudulent accounts with credit card issuer directly:
   - Provide written statement that account is fraudulent
   - Attach police report or IC3 report
   - Credit card issuer must investigate (dispute process)
5. Report to Federal Trade Commission identity theft division: 
   www.identitytheft.gov (creates recovery plan)

Issuer's Obligation:
  • Must investigate fraudulent account claim
  • Must close account if determined fraudulent
  • Should not bill you for charges before fraud determination
  • May require identity verification (police report, ID theft affidavit)

Credit Bureau Reporting:
  • Credit bureaus must report fraudulent account as "fraud" on your report
  • Fraudulent account should NOT impact your credit score
  • Fraudulent account should not appear when you apply for legitimate credit
  • If it impacts your score, you have right to dispute

6.3 CHARGEBACK PROCESS (How It Works)
──────────────────────────────────────

What is a Chargeback?
  • Issuer disputes transaction with merchant's bank
  • Merchant bank investigates and makes determination
  • Provisional credit goes to cardholder immediately
  • Takes 30-90 days typically

Chargeback Reasons (for voice phishing):
  • "Unauthorized use" - You did not authorize the charge
  • "Fraudulent transaction" - Scammer made the charge
  • "Services not rendered" - If scammer promised service/goods
  • "Merchandise not received" - If scammer promised goods

Merchant Response:
  • Merchant has right to respond to chargeback dispute
  • If merchant provides evidence of authorization, you could lose dispute
  • Note: Falling for scam alone is not defense (but authorization IS)

6.4 FRAUDULENT ACCOUNT OPENING - LEGAL RIGHTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────

Illinois Identity Theft Law:
  • 815 ILCS 530: Illinois Identity Theft Statute
  • Victims have civil right to sue for damages
  • Attorney General can prosecute criminal identity theft
  • Victims can demand identity theft affidavit from businesses

Federal Identity Theft Law:
  • 18 U.S.C. § 1028: Fraud and related activity with identification documents
  • 15 U.S.C. § 1681c: Fraud alerts and security freezes
  • FTC has authority to enforce ID theft rules

Damages You Can Claim:
  • Actual damages (unauthorized charges)
  • Statutory damages (additional compensation)
  • Reasonable costs (credit monitoring, etc.)
  • Attorney fees (if you sue)

Civil Lawsuit:
  • Sue issuer if they negligently allowed fraud
  • Sue identity thief if located/identified
  • Illinois state court or federal court (depending on claim)
  • Consult attorney for specific strategy

6.5 CREDIT MONITORING AFTER CARD FRAUD
────────────────────────────────────────

Immediate (0-7 days):
  • Change password on credit card account
  • Enable alerts for new charges
  • Contact card issuer about reissuing card
  • Receive new card in 7-10 business days

Short-term (1-30 days):
  • Place fraud alert with credit bureaus (1 year)
  • Review all open accounts for unauthorized activity
  • Check credit reports for new accounts
  • Dispute fraudulent accounts

Medium-term (1-12 months):
  • Consider credit freeze (more protective than alert)
  • Monitor credit reports monthly
  • Check bank statements weekly
  • Watch for new account opening attempts
  • Monitor tax return status (fraudsters may file false returns)

Legal References:
  - 15 U.S.C. § 1666 (Fair Credit Billing Act)
  - 815 ILCS 530 (Illinois Identity Theft Act)
  - 18 U.S.C. § 1028 (Federal ID Theft)

QUICK REFERENCE - CREDIT CARD PROTECTION
================================================================================

Unauthorized Charges:
  • Your liability: $0-$50 maximum (usually $0)
  • Report window: 60 days from statement date
  • Bank must investigate: Within 60 days
  • You get provisional credit: Immediately while investigating

Fraudulent Account Opening:
  • Not your liability (account opened without authorization)
  • Place fraud alert: Immediately with one bureau
  • Place credit freeze: Consider for maximum protection
  • Monitor credit: Monthly for 12 months

REMEMBER: Call card issuer immediately upon noticing fraudulent charge.
Liability limits only apply if you report within 60 days.
